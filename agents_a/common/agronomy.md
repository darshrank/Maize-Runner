Agronomy guidance (University of Florida IFAS, irrigated field corn; general principles, not tuned to any season):
- Nitrogen: about 1.37 lb N per bushel of expected yield, which for this field's typical yield is roughly 220-230 kg
  N/ha for the whole season. Applying more does not increase yield; it only adds cost and leaching.
- Split nitrogen into many small doses: about 34 kg N/ha at planting, about 34 kg N/ha when the corn is knee high
  (around V6), and the rest in weekly or biweekly doses, with all nitrogen applied by tassel emergence (VT).
  Nitrogen applied after tasseling is not recommended.
- If heavy rain (roughly 25 mm or more in a few days) leaches nitrogen, a rescue dose of about 34 kg N/ha is allowed.
  Avoid applying nitrogen right before heavy rain on this sandy soil: wait a few days instead.
- Water: the crop needs about 510-610 mm of water per season (rain plus irrigation). Peak use, from late vegetative
  stages through grain fill, is about 5-7 mm per day (35-50 mm per week). Even a brief drought from tasseling to the
  dough stage can cut yield by 20-50%.
- Irrigate before the crop is stressed: keep soil water above about 0.5 (half of the plant-available water) from
  the late vegetative stages through grain fill. In a dry week at peak demand, apply roughly what the crop will use
  (35-50 mm), minus the rain expected. Do not irrigate when substantial rain is expected within 2-3 days.
Sources: UF/IFAS BMP corn recommendations (bmp.ifas.ufl.edu/crop-recommendations/corn, from EDIS SS757, 2025 interim
N rate); peak water use 4-7 mm/day from Alberta Agriculture irrigation scheduling for corn; stress begins as roughly
half or more of the available soil water is depleted (University of Idaho Extension).
